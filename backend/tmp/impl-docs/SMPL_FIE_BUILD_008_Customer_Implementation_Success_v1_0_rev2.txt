A successful implementation is not one where software is installed. It is one where the customer consistently trusts, understands, and acts on the financial intelligence the platform produces. Success is measured by customer outcomes, not completed tasks.
Purpose and Authority
FIE-BUILD-001 through FIE-BUILD-007 established how the platform is engineered, integrated, released, operated, scaled, and secured. This document establishes how a customer actually gets there — discovery, solution design, implementation, validation, activation, adoption, governance, and expansion. It owns customer delivery. It redesigns nothing: ABS, GPES, BIS, FIE, and BUILD-001 through BUILD-007 remain authoritative.

Operational Philosophy
Customers experience one implementation methodology regardless of whether their data arrives via live connector (Path B), CSV upload (Path A), or a mix of both (Path C) — the direct customer-facing consequence of ABS-012's Canonical Convergence principle. Implementation differences disappear the moment data reaches Canonical; everything from Section 8 onward is identical regardless of which path got a customer there.
Customer Success Principle
A successful implementation is one in which the customer consistently trusts, understands, and acts upon the platform's financial intelligence. Success is measured by sustained business outcomes, not completed implementation tasks.
The customer-facing counterpart to FIE-BUILD-005's Operational Confidence and FIE-BUILD-007's Trust Integrity Principle — the same north-star pattern, now stated for the customer relationship itself rather than for the platform's internals. Every section that follows in this document answers to this principle: an implementation that hits every Section 2 lifecycle milestone on schedule but leaves the customer unable to trust or act on the output has not succeeded, regardless of how clean the project plan looked.
Section 1 — Customer Success Philosophy
Customer outcomes — success is measured by whether the customer trusts and acts on the platform's output, not by whether a checklist was completed
Evidence before trust — the platform's own defining principle (FIE-005), applied to the relationship: a customer is never asked to simply believe a number is right, they are shown why
Implementation transparency — a customer always knows what stage they're in, what's blocking progress, and what happens next, per Section 2's lifecycle
Long-term partnership — implementation does not end at go-live; Sections 12–14 exist because adoption, governance, and expansion are ongoing relationships, not a project close-out
Continuous adoption — the goal is a customer using more of the platform more deeply over time (Section 12, the Customer Maturity Model below), not a static, one-time deployment

Section 3 — Implementation Paths
Path A (CSV), Path B (Connector), and Path C (Hybrid) are defined in full by ABS-012 and are not redefined here. This section exists only to confirm that customer-facing implementation planning selects a path per ABS-012 §1.4's per-domain rule — never assumes one path for an entire customer — and that the guardrail against over-prescribing a connector when a customer lacks API access (ABS-012 §9) is a Discovery-stage (Section 4) responsibility, not an afterthought.

Section 11a — Customer Security Enablement
FIE-BUILD-007 explicitly deferred customer-facing security documentation and security-questionnaire responses to this document (FIE-BUILD-007 §Principal Software Architect Review, Q2) — this section is where that responsibility actually lives, rather than remaining an unclaimed gap between the two documents.
DPA timing — the customer's Data Processing Agreement (FIE-BUILD-007 §11a) is executed during Discovery (Section 4) or Implementation Readiness (Section 6), never deferred until after data has already begun flowing
Subprocessor disclosure pack — a customer-facing document listing every subprocessor, including LLM providers (FIE-BUILD-007 §11b), assembled once and kept current rather than answered ad hoc per customer request
Standard questionnaire response workflow — a maintained set of vetted answers to common enterprise security questionnaires (SOC 2, ISO 27001 status, encryption posture, per FIE-BUILD-007 §5, §8), reused and customized per customer rather than answered from scratch each time
Ownership — jointly owned by Customer Success and Security Engineering; Customer Success owns the relationship and timing, Security Engineering owns the accuracy of every answer
Timing in lifecycle — this work happens at Discovery and Implementation Readiness (Sections 4, 6), not as a pre-activation surprise at Section 9's Customer Validation gate

Customer Decision Records
Just as engineering has Engineering Decision Records (FIE-BUILD-002) and this series has since added Scalability Decision Records (FIE-BUILD-006) and Security Decision Records (FIE-BUILD-007, always spelled in full there rather than abbreviated, to avoid colliding with FIE-BUILD-006's own “SDR”), customers accumulate their own significant decisions worth preserving permanently:
Operating Model selection
Metric definitions
Module activation
Connector choices
Restatement policy (FIE-002 Addendum A)
Governance decisions
Every Customer Decision Record additionally carries:
Decision Owner — who made the call
Approval Authority — who signed off, where different from the owner
Business Justification — why, in the customer's own terms
Implementation Date
Review Date — when this decision is next revisited
Superseded By — a reference to the Customer Decision Record that replaced this one, where applicable, preserving the chain rather than leaving a dangling reference
This is the fourth Decision Record pattern in the series — all four share the same shape (problem, alternatives considered, decision, rationale, impact, owner, date), differing only in scope and filing location. Customer Decision Records are filed specifically into Organizational Financial Memory (FIE-004), as Decision Knowledge Objects — not a separate store, since a customer's own decision about their Operating Model is exactly the kind of organizational knowledge FIE-004 already exists to preserve. The additional fields above keep the decision traceable throughout the full customer lifecycle, not only at the moment it was made.

Principal Software Architect Review
Reviewed for whether customer delivery preserves every trust guarantee established throughout the architecture, not only whether implementations complete on schedule. This round incorporates four Cursor fixes (Customer Security Enablement, Path A mapping confirmation, prototype cutover pointer, go-live cohort alignment) and eleven refinements from this document's own Principal Software Architect Review.
1. Are Any Customer Implementation Responsibilities Still Misplaced?
No. This document deliberately restates, from the customer's vantage point, work already fully specified elsewhere — FIE-BUILD-003 §7's Customer Activation pipeline (Section 8), FIE-BUILD-003 §7's Customer Go-Live Validation and FIE-BUILD-001's Customer Readiness (Section 9), FIE-BUILD-005 §6's Operational Confidence and FIE-BUILD-007's Trust Integrity (Section 15). Section 11a closes the one genuine gap this round identified — FIE-BUILD-007 had explicitly deferred customer-facing security enablement here, and it was previously absent; it is now owned explicitly, jointly by Customer Success and Security Engineering. Customer Success Debt, added this round, mirrors Technical/Operational/Performance/Governance Debt exactly rather than inventing a competing mechanism.
2. Does Any Implementation Content Belong in ABS-012 or GPES?
No — Section 3 explicitly defers to ABS-012 for Path A/B/C definition and declines to redefine it, the correct boundary
GPES-001 remains the Path B reference trace (per FIE-BUILD-001 §9's clarification); this document's Section 2 lifecycle and Section 8 execution sequence are the customer-facing narrative wrapped around that trace, not a competing specification of it
One item still worth naming: the brief's cross-reference list mentions a “GPES-002” that does not otherwise appear in this documentation series — if that document exists, it should be cited specifically where relevant; if it does not yet exist, this reference should be corrected to GPES-001 the next time this document is revised
3. Does Every Implementation Activity Preserve Operational Confidence and Trust Integrity?
Yes, by direct inheritance rather than restatement. This document adds no new calculation, storage, or security mechanism of its own — every guarantee a customer experiences (determinism, evidence, lineage, confidence, immutability) is produced by FIE-001 through FIE-005 and protected by FIE-BUILD-005 through 007. Section 14's Expansion Readiness Criteria now makes this an explicit gate rather than an implicit expectation: Operational Confidence and Trust Integrity must both hold, measurably, before any expansion conversation begins.
4. Is BUILD-008 Ready to Freeze as the Authoritative Customer Implementation & Success Handbook?
Yes, with one open item still named rather than silently resolved: Section 19's reference to "BUILD-000" requires an explicit confirmation of intent — BUILD Series Overview or Engineering Handbook — before that document is drafted, per Recommendation 10. Every one of the nineteen required sections, all five original additional recommendations, and all eleven refinements from this review round are present, and every mechanism traces back to a specific guarantee already established in ABS/GPES/BIS/FIE or a prior BUILD document. Recommend freezing FIE-BUILD-008 pending confirmation of the BUILD-000 naming question, and proceeding to BUILD-000 only once that confirmation is made.
