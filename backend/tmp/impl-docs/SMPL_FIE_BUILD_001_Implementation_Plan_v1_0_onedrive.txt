This is a planning document, not an architecture document. Nothing here redesigns a frozen service. Where architecture and this plan appear to disagree, the architecture is correct and this plan is stale.
Scope note: Phases 0–1 build the Implementation Foundation (BIS-001–006) this plan depends on but does not own architecturally. FIE-BUILD-001 owns the sequencing of Phases 2–6 (the Financial Intelligence Engine itself) and Phase 7 (cutover) — Phases 0–1 are included here because nothing in Phase 2 onward can be demonstrated without them.
Scope and Ground Rules
All architecture — GPES-001, ABS-009 through ABS-012, BIS-001 through BIS-006, FIE-000 through FIE-005, and FIE-002 Addendum A — is treated as frozen. This document sequences and derisks building it; it does not change what any of it says.
Nothing in BIS-001 through BIS-006 or FIE-001 through FIE-005 is assumed to exist as running code today. Every service is planned from zero.
The existing prototype layer (CSV warehouse tables such as actual_mrr_waterfall, workspace ingest, board/outlook dashboards) is real, live, and currently the only thing customers see. It is not the target architecture, per ABS-012 §10, and is retired only on the schedule in Section 18 — never assumed away before then.
This roadmap is phase-based with exit criteria, not calendar-committed. Section 14 and Section 19 define what must be true to move to the next phase; converting that into calendar dates requires team size and velocity inputs this document does not have.

Section 13 — What Cannot Be Mocked
These are the properties that are prohibitively expensive to retrofit, per this documentation set's own repeated experience — every one of them was singled out in its owning document specifically because building it wrong the first time would be a schema migration, not a config change.
Canonical Convergence itself (BIS-003) — every downstream zero-impact claim depends on this being real from day one, not simulated
Immutability and append-only versioning, everywhere it's specified — FIE-001 Observations/Revisions, FIE-002 methodology versions, FIE-003 Financial Intelligence Objects, FIE-004 Knowledge Objects, FIE-005 Narratives. Retrofitting this after building an editable-in-place shortcut has, in this platform's own design history, repeatedly turned out to be the most expensive category of mistake to fix
The three/four-dimensional confidence model — collapsing it to a single score 'for now' and trying to re-expand it later loses information that cannot be reconstructed after the fact
FIE-002's Financial Operating Model freeze-on-creation — this is the entire reproducibility guarantee; a live-resolving stand-in would silently violate FIE-003's determinism guarantee
FIE-005's fail-closed Verification — even in a POC, a Narrative that publishes without passing Verification defeats the platform's central 'radical transparency' premise before a single real customer sees it

Section 18 — Production Cutover Strategy
18.1 Retirement of Prototype Warehouse Tables
Aggregate tables such as actual_mrr_waterfall are retired per customer, not platform-wide in one cutover event — a customer moves off the prototype only once their own data has been re-onboarded through Canonical (BIS-003) and their Milestone 6 POC has been validated end to end. Per ABS-012 §10, these tables become FIE-003-derived Reporting outputs going forward; the retirement is the point at which BIS-006 stops reading the prototype table and starts reading FIE-003 Financial Intelligence Objects for that customer.
18.2 Canonical Becomes Production Source of Truth
For each customer, Canonical (BIS-003) becomes authoritative the moment their onboarding completes Milestone 1's exit criteria. Historical prototype data does not retroactively become canonical — per ABS-012 §8.2's honest continuity limit, canonical history for that customer begins at onboarding, with a documented discontinuity before it. This is a business communication point, not only an engineering one — see 18.6.
18.3 FIE-003 Produces Financial Intelligence Objects
Once a customer's Financial Operating Model is composed (Milestone 3) and their first Calculation Run completes (Milestone 4), FIE-003 FIOs become the authoritative source for every metric the prototype previously served directly from actual_mrr_waterfall-style tables for that customer.
18.4 Reporting Consumes FIE Outputs
BIS-006 is repointed per customer from the prototype's direct table read to FIE-005 Narrative Objects and FIE-003 FIOs, per FIE-000 §11. This is a per-customer configuration change, not a platform-wide code branch — both paths can coexist in BIS-006 during the transition window.
18.5 Rollback Strategy
Per-customer feature flag controlling whether BIS-006 reads from the prototype table or from FIE-003/FIE-005 — cutover is reversible per customer, not all-or-nothing
The prototype tables and their loading scripts are not deleted at cutover — they are marked deprecated and retained until a defined post-cutover confidence window elapses, per the platform's universal no-deletion posture
Any discrepancy detected between the prototype's numbers and FIE-003's numbers during the parallel-run window blocks cutover for that customer until reconciled and explained — never silently resolved by preferring one source
18.6 Dependency on ABS-015
BLOCKED  ABS-015 does not exist yet. This plan recommends it be scoped as a dedicated Customer Migration / Prototype Retirement Standard, covering: the exact per-customer cutover checklist implied by 18.1–18.5, customer communication templates for the honest discontinuity point in 18.2, and the formal criteria for when a customer's deprecated prototype data may finally be archived. It should be authored before Milestone 7 work begins in earnest, not during it.
18.7 Customer Migration Considerations
Customers should be told plainly, before cutover, that canonical history starts at their onboarding date — this is ABS-012 §8.2's honest-discontinuity principle applied to customer communication, not just internal documentation
A parallel-run period (both prototype and FIE-derived numbers visible) is recommended per customer before the prototype is fully deprecated for them, specifically to catch discrepancies per 18.5
Customers with data only ever loaded into aggregate prototype tables (never canonical) require a full re-onboarding, not a lightweight migration, per ABS-012 §10's own findings

Customer Readiness
The platform is not successful simply because the code works. A customer must also be operationally ready to trust and rely on it — this section is deliberately about trust, not engineering completion, and sits alongside Section 19's milestones rather than inside them.
Data onboarding complete — the customer's source data has been fully mapped and validated through Canonical, per Milestone 1
Financial Operating Model approved — the customer's finance team has reviewed and approved the specific methodology versions in production use, not just accepted a default
Metric definitions approved — the customer agrees with how ARR, Bookings, and every other reported concept is defined for their business
Executive review completed — a CFO or finance leader has reviewed real output, not a demo with sample data
Variance review completed — at least one real variance narrative has been reviewed and confirmed accurate by the customer
Board package validated — if applicable, a board-facing output has been reviewed by whoever actually presents to the board
Reporting approved — the customer has confirmed the rendered output (Section 4's golden path) meets their presentation needs
Customer training complete — the people who will use this day to day know how to inspect evidence, confidence, and lineage, not just read a narrative
Customer signs off that outputs are trusted — an explicit, named sign-off, not an inferred one from silence
None of these are engineering deliverables. All of them are prerequisites this plan treats as seriously as any Milestone 19 exit criterion — a technically complete Milestone 6 with no Customer Readiness sign-off is not yet a successful POC.

Closing Note
This plan sequences seven phases and seven milestones across nineteen sections, and every dependency claim in it traces to a specific section of a frozen document — nothing here was invented to make the roadmap look tidier than the architecture actually is. The two BLOCKED items (ABS-013, ABS-015) are named explicitly rather than quietly assumed away, because the alternative — discovering mid-build that a milestone depends on a document that was never written — is exactly the failure mode this entire documentation series has spent nineteen prior documents avoiding.
If team size and velocity become known, Section 14's phases and Section 19's milestones convert directly into calendar dates without needing to be resequenced — the dependency structure is already complete; only the arithmetic is missing.
