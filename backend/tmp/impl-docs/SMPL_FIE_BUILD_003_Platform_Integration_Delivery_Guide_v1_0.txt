Platform Assembly Before Platform Scale. The platform must operate correctly for one customer before it is optimized for one thousand.
Purpose and Authority
FIE-BUILD-001 established WHAT gets built and in what order. FIE-BUILD-002 established HOW each engineer builds each service consistently. This document establishes HOW the assembled whole becomes one working, operable system — environments, infrastructure, startup order, integration testing, cutover, monitoring, failure recovery, disaster recovery, deployment, certification, and customer acceptance. It redesigns nothing: ABS, BIS, FIE, and both prior BUILD documents remain frozen and authoritative. Every recommendation here maps back to a specific section of one of them.

A service can be fully coded (per FIE-BUILD-002) and still be undeployable if the infrastructure and services below it in this stack aren't live yet — this is the distinction between 'built' and 'assembled.'

BIS-001 (Connector Proxy) and BIS-005 (Knowledge Platform) are not shown in the critical startup chain above — per FIE-BUILD-001 §3, BIS-001 may be satisfied by a fixture/CSV loader at POC, and BIS-005's minimum slice has no hard startup-order dependency on anything above it. Check
Behavior
Health checks
Every service's GET /v1/health (per its own API section) must return healthy before the next service in the chain is started
Readiness
Distinct from health, per FIE-BUILD-002 §11 — a service may be alive but not yet ready (e.g. still running migrations); the startup sequence waits on readiness, not just health
Dependency validation
Each service validates on startup that every service below it in this chain is reachable and healthy before accepting traffic — failing this check keeps the service in a not-ready state rather than accepting requests it cannot fulfill
Failure behavior
A failure to start in dependency order halts the chain at that point — later services are never started against a not-yet-healthy dependency, and the failure is surfaced immediately rather than discovered on first request

Reference: ABS-015 (Customer Migration / Prototype Retirement Standard) does not exist yet, per FIE-BUILD-001 §18.6 — the per-customer checklist below is this document's best current operational interpretation of what ABS-015 should formalize, not a substitute for it.
Parallel runs: both the prototype and the FIE-derived pipeline produce output for the same customer and period simultaneously, for a defined window before cutover, per FIE-BUILD-001 §18.5

Section 11 — Disaster Recovery
Backups: automated, regular backups of every service's Postgres store, retained per the platform's data retention policy
Restore: a documented, tested restore procedure per service, exercised on a schedule, not only when an actual disaster occurs
Point-in-time recovery: available for every service's database, exploiting the platform's own append-only, versioned data model (FIE-BUILD-002 §8) — a PITR restore of an immutable, versioned store is inherently safer than restoring a mutable one, since there is no in-place edit history to lose partial context on
Infrastructure loss: documented recovery runbook per infrastructure component (Section 3), with clear ownership per FIE-BUILD-002's Engineering Ownership Matrix (FIE-BUILD-001)
Region failure: if the platform operates in multiple regions, documented failover procedure; if single-region at current scale, documented recovery time objective and communicated honestly as a current limitation, not silently assumed away
Data corruption: because the platform is append-only and versioned everywhere (FIE-BUILD-002 §8), a corruption event's blast radius is bounded to the specific rows affected — recovery is restoring or replaying from the last known-good version, never reconstructing a mutable table's edit history
Deployment failure: rollback per Section 2's per-environment rollback rules and FIE-BUILD-002 §12's rollback strategy

Section 13 — Platform Certification
The honest answer to "can we call SMPL production ready" — not per-service completeness (FIE-BUILD-002 §13's Definition of Done), but whole-platform readiness.
Canonical complete — BIS-003 operational across every domain the customer set requires
Validation complete — BIS-004 rules cover every domain's structural and business-rule checks in scope
Observation complete — FIE-001 bi-temporal capture and Snapshot completeness verified against real customer data
Methodology complete — FIE-002 core and, where needed, Addendum A operational and tested
Calculation complete — FIE-003 determinism and lineage verified per FIE-BUILD-002 Appendix A's FIE-003 additions
Reasoning complete — FIE-005 Verification fail-closed behavior and Explainability Adapter contract verified
Reporting complete — BIS-006 rendering the actual customer's Narratives and FIOs, computing nothing itself
Security complete — per FIE-BUILD-002 §10, reviewed against Section 19's PR governance gates across every service in scope
Monitoring complete — Section 9's full dashboard and alert suite live, not just individual service dashboards
Performance complete — every in-scope service meets its own Performance Targets section, and the platform-level directional targets (FIE-BUILD-001) are tracked even where not yet fully met
Customer trust complete — Section 14's acceptance questions resolve affirmatively for at least one real, live customer
Certification is granted per customer cohort, not as a single platform-wide switch — a platform can be legitimately 'production ready' for a narrow Path A ARR use case (FIE-BUILD-001's golden path) well before it is production ready for full Path B, multi-domain, multi-methodology customers.
Section 14 — Customer Acceptance
Every production deployment answers these eight questions before it is considered complete. This restates, at the platform-integration level, FIE-BUILD-001's Product Acceptance Criteria and FIE-BUILD-002's Engineering Trust Checklist — the same underlying trust principle, checked once more at the point a real deployment actually reaches a real customer, not only during development.
Can customers trust this? — Section 13's certification for their specific cohort has been granted
Can customers verify this? — FIE-005's Verification passed, and the Explainability Adapter resolves for every value they can see
Can customers explain this? — every published value answers FIE-BUILD-002 §17's nine Explainability questions
Can customers reproduce this? — FIE-003's determinism guarantee holds for their Operating Model version
Can customers reconcile this? — the Financial Tie-Out pattern (FIE-005 §15) resolves end to end for their data
Can customers audit this? — every governed decision (methodology approval, restatement, promotion) is logged per FIE-BUILD-002 §10
Can customers restate this? — where FIE-002 Addendum A applies, RESTATEMENT_COMPARISON (Addendum A §7) resolves correctly
Can customers understand this? — their Narrative Policy (FIE-005 §18) produces output in language appropriate to their actual audience, not generic output

New Principle: Platform Assembly Before Platform Scale
Correctness before scale. Repeatability before optimization.
This principle has been implicit throughout the BUILD series and is made explicit here because platform integration is where the temptation to violate it is strongest. It would be easy, once eleven services exist, to immediately optimize for one thousand customers — parallelize everything, cache aggressively, shard early. This document's own sequencing rejects that: FIE-BUILD-001's golden path proves the platform correct for one customer (Milestone 6) before FIE-BUILD-003's certification (Section 13) considers broader readiness, and Section 9's monitoring exists to prove repeatable correctness before Section 12's pipeline is trusted to deploy faster and more often.
Concretely: a platform that handles one customer's ARR narrative correctly, reproducibly, and explainably is a stronger foundation than a platform that handles a thousand customers' narratives quickly but with an unresolved confidence-propagation edge case. Every optimization in this document — caching (Section 3), parallel execution (referenced from FIE-003), scaled worker pools (Section 3) — is sequenced after the correctness properties (determinism, immutability, explainability) they would otherwise be tempted to trade away.
