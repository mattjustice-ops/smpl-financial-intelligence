July 2026 · CONFIDENTIAL — CUSTOMER IMPLEMENTATION HANDBOOK
A successful implementation is not one where software is installed. It is one where the customer consistently trusts, understands, and acts on the financial intelligence the platform produces. Success is measured by customer outcomes, not completed tasks.
Purpose and Authority
FIE-BUILD-001 through FIE-BUILD-007 established how the platform is engineered, integrated, released, operated, scaled, and secured. This document establishes how a customer actually gets there — discovery, solution design, implementation, validation, activation, adoption, governance, and expansion. It owns customer delivery. It redesigns nothing: ABS, GPES, BIS, FIE, and BUILD-001 through BUILD-007 remain authoritative.

Operational Philosophy
Customers experience one implementation methodology regardless of whether their data arrives via live connector (Path B), CSV upload (Path A), or a mix of both (Path C) — the direct customer-facing consequence of ABS-012's Canonical Convergence principle. Implementation differences disappear the moment data reaches Canonical; everything from Section 8 onward is identical regardless of which path got a customer there.
Section 1 — Customer Success Philosophy
Customer outcomes — success is measured by whether the customer trusts and acts on the platform's output, not by whether a checklist was completed
Evidence before trust — the platform's own defining principle (FIE-005), applied to the relationship: a customer is never asked to simply believe a number is right, they are shown why
Implementation transparency — a customer always knows what stage they're in, what's blocking progress, and what happens next, per Section 2's lifecycle
Long-term partnership — implementation does not end at go-live; Sections 12–14 exist because adoption, governance, and expansion are ongoing relationships, not a project close-out
Continuous adoption — the goal is a customer using more of the platform more deeply over time (Section 12, the Customer Maturity Model below), not a static, one-time deployment

Section 3 — Implementation Paths
Path A (CSV), Path B (Connector), and Path C (Hybrid) are defined in full by ABS-012 and are not redefined here. This section exists only to confirm that customer-facing implementation planning selects a path per ABS-012 §1.4's per-domain rule — never assumes one path for an entire customer — and that the guardrail against over-prescribing a connector when a customer lacks API access (ABS-012 §9) is a Discovery-stage (Section 4) responsibility, not an afterthought.
Section 4 — Customer Discovery
Business objectives — what the customer actually needs the platform to answer
Financial concepts — which of the customer's metrics matter most (ARR, cash, GTM efficiency, and so on)
Current reporting — what the customer does today, and where it breaks down
Source systems — per domain, feeding ABS-012's per-domain access question directly
Security — customer-specific requirements surfaced early, feeding FIE-BUILD-007's Section 11a/11b where relevant (data residency, subprocessor restrictions)
Stakeholders — who approves what, feeding Section 13's governance model
Timeline — realistic, set against Section 16's Implementation Duration KPI, not an arbitrary date
Success criteria — defined before implementation begins, not retrofitted at the end
This is the customer-facing counterpart to ABS-009's CEP discovery principle ("if AI can discover it, don't ask") — Discovery asks only what genuinely cannot be inferred from connected or uploaded data.

Connector strategy — Path A/B/C per domain, per Section 3
Governance — which approval workflows (FIE-BUILD-007 §4) apply to this customer's elevated actions
Implementation roadmap — the customer-specific sequencing of Section 2's lifecycle stages
Section 6 — Implementation Readiness
A pre-implementation gate — distinct from, and earlier than, FIE-BUILD-001's Customer Readiness (which is the trust sign-off gate at the end of the golden path, Section 9–below). This section asks whether the customer is ready to begin; FIE-BUILD-001's Customer Readiness asks whether the resulting output can be trusted.

Usage — actual login and feature-use frequency, not licenses purchased

Executive engagement — whether leadership, not only finance staff, uses the platform directly
Operational maturity — the customer's position on the Customer Maturity Model, below

Customer Trust Journey
Imported        (data has arrived, nothing yet validated)     ↓Validated       (BIS-004 checks pass, per Section 9)     ↓Trusted         (customer signoff recorded, per Section 9)     ↓Operational     (steady-state adoption, per Section 12)     ↓Strategic       (executive engagement, per Section 12)     ↓Transformational (the platform has changed how the customer actually runs finance)
Customers mature through these stages in order — a customer cannot be "Strategic" while still merely "Imported" for a given domain. This journey is the trust-specific lens on Section 2's broader lifecycle.

Success Gates
Before advancing a customer to the next lifecycle stage:
Discovery Complete       (Section 4)     ↓Canonical Validated      (Section 8)     ↓Financial Validation     (Section 9)     ↓Operational Confidence   (Section 9, FIE-BUILD-005 §6)     ↓Executive Approval       (Section 9)     ↓Production               (Section 10)
Every stage has objective exit criteria — a customer never advances on a target date alone if the gate above it hasn't actually been satisfied, the same fail-closed discipline this platform applies to its own technical execution, applied here to the customer relationship.
Customer Decision Records
Just as engineering has Engineering Decision Records (FIE-BUILD-002) and this series has since added Scalability Decision Records (FIE-BUILD-006) and Security Decision Records (FIE-BUILD-007), customers accumulate their own significant decisions worth preserving permanently:
Operating Model selection
Metric definitions
Module activation
Connector choices
Restatement policy (FIE-002 Addendum A)
Governance decisions
This is the fourth Decision Record pattern in the series — all four share the same shape (problem, alternatives considered, decision, rationale, impact, owner, date), differing only in scope and filing location. Customer Decision Records are filed specifically into Organizational Financial Memory (FIE-004), as Decision Knowledge Objects — not a separate store, since a customer's own decision about their Operating Model is exactly the kind of organizational knowledge FIE-004 already exists to preserve.

Principal Software Architect Review
Reviewed for whether customer delivery preserves every trust guarantee established throughout the architecture, not only whether implementations complete on schedule.
1. Are Any Customer Implementation Responsibilities Misplaced?
No. This document deliberately restates, from the customer's vantage point, work already fully specified elsewhere — FIE-BUILD-003 §7's Customer Activation pipeline (Section 8), FIE-BUILD-003 §7's Customer Go-Live Validation and FIE-BUILD-001's Customer Readiness (Section 9), FIE-BUILD-005 §6's Operational Confidence and FIE-BUILD-007's Trust Integrity (Section 15). None of these is redefined; each is cited to its owning document and given a customer-facing narrative around it.
2. Does Any Implementation Content Belong in ABS-012 or GPES?
No — Section 3 explicitly defers to ABS-012 for Path A/B/C definition and declines to redefine it, the correct boundary
GPES-001 remains the Path B reference trace (per FIE-BUILD-001 §9's clarification); this document's Section 2 lifecycle and Section 8 execution sequence are the customer-facing narrative wrapped around that trace, not a competing specification of it
One item worth naming: the brief's cross-reference list mentions a “GPES-002” that does not otherwise appear in this documentation series — if that document exists, it should be cited specifically where relevant; if it does not yet exist, this reference should be corrected to GPES-001 the next time this document is revised
3. Does BUILD-008 Preserve Every Trust Guarantee Established Throughout the Architecture?
Yes, by direct inheritance rather than restatement. This document adds no new calculation, storage, or security mechanism of its own — every guarantee a customer experiences (determinism, evidence, lineage, confidence, immutability) is produced by FIE-001 through FIE-005 and protected by FIE-BUILD-005 through 007; this document's job is making sure the customer actually sees and trusts what those layers already guarantee, per Section 1's philosophy that evidence must come before trust in the relationship itself, not only in the platform's internals.
4. Is BUILD-008 Ready to Become the Authoritative Customer Implementation & Success Handbook?
Yes, with one open item named rather than silently resolved: Section 19's reference to "BUILD-000" should be confirmed before that document is drafted, per the ASSUMED note above. Every one of the nineteen required sections plus all five additional recommendations (Customer Trust Journey, Customer Maturity Model, Success Gates, Customer Decision Records, Customer Success Dashboard) are present, and every mechanism traces back to a specific guarantee already established in ABS/GPES/BIS/FIE or a prior BUILD document rather than inventing new customer-facing infrastructure. Recommend freezing FIE-BUILD-008 pending confirmation of the BUILD-000 naming question.
