SMPL.ai · Platform Engineering

A platform that is online but producing incorrect financial conclusions is unhealthy. Infrastructure health alone is insufficient.
Purpose and Authority
FIE-BUILD-001 established what gets built. FIE-BUILD-002 established how engineers build each service. FIE-BUILD-003 established how services integrate into one production platform. FIE-BUILD-004 established how platform changes safely reach production. This document establishes how the production platform is continuously operated afterward — monitoring, incident response, disaster recovery, capacity, and the customer-facing operational discipline that keeps trust intact between releases, not just during them. It redefines nothing: ABS, BIS, FIE, and BUILD-001 through BUILD-004 remain authoritative. This document operationalizes them.

Section 2 — Operational Philosophy
Reliability over speed — an operational decision under pressure defaults to the safer, slower path, the same priority FIE-BUILD-004's Release Philosophy establishes for shipping
Automation first — a runbook that can be executed by a script should be, freeing human judgment for the cases that actually need it
Humans remain accountable — automation executes; a named human owns the outcome, per Section 19
Evidence before trust — an operational claim ("the platform is healthy") is only as good as the metric backing it, per Section 6
Fail closed — the same principle FIE-BUILD-002 §1 established for code applies to operations: an ambiguous signal defaults to raising an alert, not to assuming health
Customer-first operations — an incident's severity (Section 9) is classified by customer impact, not by which system happened to fail
Operational transparency — customers are told what happened, per Section 17, not left to infer it from a status page's silence
Financial correctness over infrastructure availability — a platform serving stale or unverified financial output quickly is worse than a platform correctly reporting it cannot answer yet

Section 6 — Operational Confidence Dashboard
Unique to this platform. Infrastructure monitoring alone cannot answer "can we currently trust what the platform is telling customers" — this dashboard exists specifically to answer that question, continuously, in production.
Operational Confidence is the platform's Golden Signal. While infrastructure metrics measure whether the system is up, Operational Confidence measures whether customers can currently trust the financial conclusions being produced.
Every other operational metric in this document ultimately contributes toward maintaining or improving this one signal — it is the single number Operations checks first, with every contributing factor below always one click away, never presented as an opaque score standing on its own.

Section 7 — Alerting Strategy
Alert severity maps directly to Section 9's incident severity model — an alert's configured severity is not a separate scale
Routing sends an alert to the on-call rotation (Section 10) owning the affected Platform Area, per FIE-BUILD-001's Engineering Ownership Matrix and FIE-BUILD-003's Platform Ownership Matrix
Deduplication collapses repeated firings of the same underlying condition into one active incident, never a flood of identical pages
Escalation follows Section 9's timing per severity if the primary on-call does not acknowledge within the expected window
Suppression is time-boxed and requires a reason logged against the suppressed alert — silence is never the default response to a noisy alert, fixing the alert or its threshold is
Alert ownership: every alert has a named owning discipline (Section 19), so an unowned alert is itself treated as a gap to close, not a permanent state
Noise reduction: an alert with a chronically low signal-to-action ratio is retuned or retired, per FIE-BUILD-003 §9's alert-fatigue guidance
Customer-visible alerting: a subset of alerts (severe, customer-impacting) feed directly into Section 17's status page and communication flow, automatically, not by manual relay
Financial Integrity Monitoring

Section 10 — On-call Operations
Rotations are per discipline, matching FIE-BUILD-001's Engineering Ownership Matrix and FIE-BUILD-003's Platform Ownership Matrix — Backend Engineering rotates for Canonical/Observation/Calculation/Memory issues, AI Engineering for Reasoning, and so on
Ownership: every rotation has a named primary and secondary, never an unowned gap
Escalation follows Section 9's per-severity timing
Coverage is continuous — a rotation handoff never leaves a window with no assigned on-call
Shift handoffs include a written summary of any in-flight incident or elevated-risk condition, not a verbal-only pass
Operational responsibilities during on-call include acknowledging Section 7 alerts, triaging per Section 9, and executing Section 11's runbooks where applicable
After-hours expectations are explicit and reasonable — an on-call engineer is expected to respond within Section 9's timing, not to be instantly available for SEV-4 items

Section 13 — Backup and Restore
Database backups: automated, regular, per-service, retained per the platform's data retention policy
Object storage backups: artifacts and Cold-tier archives (FIE-001 §17) backed up on the same cadence discipline
Registry backups: Methodologies, Business Themes, Narrative Policies, and every other registry are themselves append-only and versioned (FIE-BUILD-002's Versioning Philosophy) — their own history is a backup in structure, but physical backup still applies for infrastructure-loss scenarios
Configuration backups: environment configuration (FIE-BUILD-002 §5) versioned and backed up alongside code
Verification: every backup is periodically verified restorable, not just assumed to have succeeded because the job reported success
Restore testing: exercised on the same schedule as Section 12's DR testing cadence, not treated as a separate concern
Retention: matches the platform's data retention policy, consistent across every service
Recovery validation: a restored environment passes the same Golden Path check as Section 12's DR validation before being trusted
Section 14 — Capacity Management
Expands FIE-BUILD-003's Capacity Expansion Guidelines from decision points into an ongoing management practice.
Growth forecasting: customer onboarding volume and per-customer data volume projected against current infrastructure sizing, reviewed on a regular cadence
Scaling: horizontal scaling of stateless API layers and worker pools, per FIE-BUILD-002 §9, triggered by the decision points FIE-BUILD-003 already named
Performance thresholds: tracked against every service's own Performance Targets section; sustained approach toward a threshold triggers a capacity review before it is breached, not after
Queue sizing: per FIE-BUILD-003 §9's Queue Health domain, sized against observed depth trends, not a single historical peak
Database growth: tracked per FIE-001 §25's partitioning guidance and extended to any other high-volume table as it emerges
Storage growth: Cold-tier archive growth (FIE-001 §17) tracked against object storage cost and lifecycle policy
Connector scaling: per-domain, per-path ingestion capacity reviewed as customer count per path grows
Cost monitoring: infrastructure cost tracked against customer count and revenue, so scaling decisions are informed by unit economics, not only technical headroom

Section 17 — Customer Communications
Status page: real-time, reflecting Section 9 severity classifications honestly, never quietly softened
Maintenance windows: scheduled per FIE-BUILD-004's Release Calendar, communicated per that document's Release Freeze Windows discipline
Incident updates: cadence tied to Section 9 severity — SEV-1 updates are frequent, SEV-4 requires none

Section 18 — Postmortems
Blameless culture — a postmortem investigates the system and process that allowed an incident, never assigns individual fault
Evidence collection — logs, metrics, and the specific Section 6/Financial Integrity signals that fired or should have fired
Timeline — reconstructed from Section 8's incident lifecycle timestamps
Root cause analysis — the actual causal chain, not the first plausible explanation
Corrective actions — immediate fixes, tracked to completion
Preventive actions — systemic changes preventing recurrence, which may include a new Financial Integrity alert (above) if the gap was a monitoring blind spot
Architecture implications — if a postmortem reveals a genuine architectural gap, it is raised as an EDR (FIE-BUILD-002) against the relevant owning specification, never silently patched around
Engineering backlog — every preventive action becomes a tracked backlog item, feeding Section 20
Operational Debt
The operational counterpart to the Technical Debt Register already established in this documentation series (FIE-BUILD-001) — operational improvements compete for prioritization alongside engineering work, rather than remaining undocumented and perpetually deferred.
Missing runbooks — a Table E11.1 category with no linked procedure, per the P3 note above
Excessive alert noise — an alert with a chronically low signal-to-action ratio not yet retuned, per Section 7
Manual operational procedures — a runbook step that could be automated but isn't yet, per Section 2's Automation First principle
Missing dashboards — a Section 5 dashboard defined but not built
Weak monitoring coverage — a Section 4 monitoring type with known gaps
Missing ownership — an alert or runbook with no named owner, per Section 19
Incomplete operational automation — any Section 3 lifecycle stage still requiring manual intervention where automation was intended
Tracked the same way Technical Debt is tracked — with an owner and a trigger condition, not a vague aspiration to "get to eventually."

Final Engineering Review
Reviewed as a Principal Software Architect responsible for the platform's ongoing operational integrity, not just its release process.
1. Are Any Operational Responsibilities Misplaced?
No. This document deliberately expands, rather than duplicates, FIE-BUILD-003 §9 and §11 — those sections remain correct at the platform-integration level; this document is the operational depth both explicitly deferred. Section 19's governance table is stated as subordinate to the three prior ownership/governance tables (FIE-BUILD-001, FIE-BUILD-003, FIE-BUILD-004) rather than a fourth, competing one.
2. Does Any Content Belong in BUILD-006 Instead?
Formal load-testing methodology behind Section 14's capacity thresholds belongs in FIE-BUILD-006 Performance & Scalability, per the authoritative roadmap
Multi-region and high-availability architecture referenced in Section 12's regional recovery entry belongs in FIE-BUILD-006 once Platform Maturity Level 4 (FIE-BUILD-003) is actually approached
Formal audit trail and compliance certification implied by Section 19's audit requirements row belongs in FIE-BUILD-007 Security, Compliance & Governance, not as an expansion of this document
For clarity going forward: FIE-BUILD-006 expands operational performance, scalability, throughput, and capacity engineering. FIE-BUILD-007 expands operational security, compliance, governance, and audit. Neither absorbs this document's own responsibility — FIE-BUILD-005 remains the authoritative handbook governing day-to-day production platform operations; FIE-BUILD-006 and 007 extend specific dimensions of it, they do not gradually replace it.
3. Is BUILD-005 Ready to Become the Authoritative Production Operations Handbook?
Yes. Every one of the twenty-one required sections plus all three additional operational capabilities (Operational Confidence, Financial Integrity Monitoring, Explainability Monitoring) are present, every mechanism traces back to a specific principle already established in ABS/BIS/FIE or a prior BUILD document, and the two sections this document most directly extends (FIE-BUILD-003 §9, §11) are cited explicitly as the boundary being extended rather than silently duplicated. Recommend freezing FIE-BUILD-005 and proceeding to FIE-BUILD-006 (Performance & Scalability) per the authoritative roadmap.
