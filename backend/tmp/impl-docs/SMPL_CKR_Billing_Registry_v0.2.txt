Category Overview
This section establishes universal knowledge about Billing platforms before any vendor-specific documentation begins — Tier 1 foundation that every Billing connector specification in this registry builds upon, consistent with the category overview pattern established in the ERP and CRM registries.
✱ ARCHITECT NOTE: This registry is designed to be consumed equally well by human implementation consultants and AI implementation agents. Throughout this document, the implementation agent evaluates connector readiness, the Connector Engine maps vendor objects to canonical targets, the Capability Assessment Layer determines module status, and the CEP requests customer confirmation for ambiguous configuration.
What Defines a Billing Platform
A Billing platform is the operational system of record for recurring commercial execution — the system that actually charges customers, generates invoices, processes payments, and tracks the moment-by-moment state of every subscription. Where ERP explains financial truth (what was recognized as revenue under GAAP) and CRM explains commercial intent (what the sales team believes will happen), Billing explains commercial execution: what is actually happening to a customer's subscription right now, and what cash has actually moved as a result.
Every Billing platform in this registry maintains, at minimum, a Customer object, a Subscription object representing an active recurring commitment, an Invoice object representing a billing event, and a Payment object representing cash collected. Beyond this core, Billing platforms extend into usage metering, dunning and collections workflows, proration logic, and — in the more financially sophisticated platforms — revenue recognition scheduling that overlaps meaningfully with ERP functionality.
For SMPL, Billing is the primary connector category for ARR and MRR intelligence. No ERP can produce trustworthy subscription-level ARR Movement data — ERPs record GL-level financial postings, not subscription state changes. No CRM can confirm that a sales-recorded Closed Won deal actually became a paying, active subscription — CRMs record sales intent, not billing execution. Billing is the only connector category that can answer, with operational authority: is this customer's subscription active, and how much are they paying right now?
How Billing Differs From ERP and CRM — and How All Three Complement Each Other
The three domains answer related but distinct questions about the same underlying business activity. ERP answers: what revenue have we recognized under GAAP, and what does our balance sheet show? CRM answers: what are we trying to sell, and how likely is it to close? Billing answers: what is a customer's subscription actually doing right now, and what cash has it actually generated?
None of the three domains can substitute for either of the others. ARR computed purely from Billing subscription data is operationally accurate but is not GAAP revenue — invoice timing and service-period amortization are an ERP concern. Bookings recorded purely in CRM are sales intent, not confirmed subscriptions — a Closed Won Opportunity does not guarantee a Billing system ever created the corresponding Subscription. Revenue recognized purely in the ERP, without Billing's subscription-level detail, cannot explain which specific subscriptions or customers drove a revenue change.
SMPL's Financial Intelligence depends on connecting all three domains. The canonical ARR Movement object — the single most consequential object in the entire SCBM for SaaS companies — is most reliably populated from Billing data, validated against CRM's booking record, and reconciled against ERP's recognized revenue. This three-way reconciliation is the foundation of trusted Financial Intelligence, and Billing is the domain that makes the middle term of that reconciliation possible.
Subscription Lifecycle Management
Every Billing platform models a Subscription as a stateful object moving through a defined lifecycle: Trial (if applicable) → Active → (Paused, optionally) → (Amended: upgraded, downgraded, or add-ons changed, any number of times) → Renewed (any number of times) → Cancelled or Churned. The specific state names and allowed transitions vary by vendor, but this general lifecycle shape is universal across every platform in this registry.
Trial: a time-bounded period of access before the first paid billing cycle, which may or may not require a payment method on file depending on vendor and customer configuration.
Plan upgrades and downgrades: a change in the subscribed plan tier, typically triggering proration of the current billing period.
Add-on management: the addition or removal of supplementary subscription items (seats, modules, usage allowances) without changing the base plan.
Subscription amendments: any mid-cycle change to a subscription's terms — quantity, plan, price, or billing frequency — that is not a full upgrade or downgrade.
Pauses and resumes: a temporary suspension of billing (and often product access) without cancelling the underlying subscription record, used for seasonal businesses or customer-requested holds.
Reactivations: the resumption of a previously cancelled subscription, typically after a dormancy period — the Billing-side trigger for the canonical Reactivation ARR Movement category.
Cancellations: the termination of a subscription, either immediately or at the end of the current billing period — the Billing-side trigger for the canonical Churn ARR Movement category.
Invoice Lifecycle
An Invoice moves from Draft (in some platforms) or Open, to Paid, Partially Paid, Void, or Uncollectible. Invoice generation is typically automated on a recurring schedule tied to the subscription's billing cycle, with proration applied automatically when a subscription change occurs mid-cycle. Credit Memos and Refunds are typically modeled as separate objects that reduce a customer's net billed amount without altering the original Invoice record.
Payment Lifecycle and Collections
A Payment is an attempt to collect funds against an Invoice, which may succeed, fail, or require retry. Failed payments trigger Dunning — an automated sequence of retry attempts and customer communications designed to recover a failed payment before the subscription is suspended or cancelled for non-payment. Collections management extends this further for invoiced (non-card) payment methods, where Accounts Receivable aging and manual collection follow-up become relevant — an area of meaningful overlap with ERP-side AR functionality.
Usage-Based and Metered Billing
Usage-based billing charges customers based on consumption — API calls, seats actively used, data processed — rather than (or in addition to) a fixed recurring fee. A Meter defines what is being measured; Usage Records capture the actual consumption events; the Billing platform aggregates usage against the customer's plan (which may include a committed minimum, a per-unit overage rate, or both) to determine the billed amount. Usage-based billing is the single largest source of ARR classification ambiguity across every Billing connector in this registry — committed minimums are reasonably treated as ARR, while uncommitted overages are not, and the boundary between the two is a customer-specific configuration decision, never a platform default.
Proration and Billing Frequency
Proration calculates a partial charge or credit when a subscription changes mid-billing-cycle — upgrading from a monthly to an annual plan halfway through a month, for example. Billing Frequency (monthly, quarterly, annual, or custom) determines the cadence of invoice generation and is itself a customer-configurable subscription attribute, not a platform-level setting.
Billing vs. ERP — Where the Boundary Sits
Billing platforms generate invoices and collect cash; they do not, on their own, determine when revenue should be recognized under ASC 606 / IFRS 15. Some Billing platforms (Zuora, and to a lesser extent Chargebee and Maxio) offer native revenue recognition scheduling that meaningfully overlaps with ERP functionality — where this exists, it is documented per-connector as a confidence-improving capability, never as a substitute for ERP-confirmed recognized revenue. The canonical ownership principle stated in the Cross-Domain Relationships section below is binding throughout this registry: Billing contributes a Revenue Schedule reference where its native recognition functionality exists, but the ERP remains the canonical owner of recognized revenue.
Billing vs. CRM — Where the Boundary Sits
CRM records sales intent; Billing records commercial execution. A Closed Won Opportunity in CRM does not guarantee a corresponding Subscription was ever created in Billing — sales and billing systems can and do fall out of sync, particularly for usage-based or negotiated enterprise deals where manual provisioning introduces a gap between contract signature and billing system setup. Every Billing connector specification in this registry documents this gap explicitly as a Common Failure Mode, and the canonical resolution — bookings confirmation via CRM+Billing reconciliation — is documented in Connector Synergies for every connector.

The purchase of supplementary subscription items alongside a base plan.

Charging based on measured consumption rather than a fixed fee.
The single largest ARR classification ambiguity in this registry — committed minimums are reasonably ARR; uncommitted overages are not; the line is a CEP-configurable decision, never a platform default.
Proration
Partial charges or credits calculated for mid-cycle subscription changes.
Proration methodology (exact day-count vs. flat half-period) varies by platform and affects the precision of mid-cycle ARR Movement amounts.
Contract vs. Subscription
The distinction between a signed commercial agreement and its operational billing representation.
A single Contract may govern multiple Subscriptions (e.g., a multi-product deal); the relationship between the two is frequently not natively modeled in Billing platforms and may require CRM or Contract-object cross-reference.
Bookings vs. Billings
Bookings is the total contract value committed; Billings is what has actually been invoiced to date.
For annual-prepay contracts, Billings can lag or lead Bookings significantly — the two terms are never interchangeable, and Billing platforms natively report Billings, not Bookings (which is primarily a CRM/Contract concept).
ARR vs. MRR
ARR is the annualized view; MRR is the monthly view of the same committed recurring value.
Per SCBM D-013 and the SCBM canonical glossary, ARR is the primary reporting metric at SMPL; MRR is a derived field (ARR ÷ 12), never independently computed or stored.
Deferred Revenue Relationships
The balance sheet liability representing cash collected (or billed) but not yet recognized as revenue.
Billing platforms with native revenue recognition can contribute a Deferred Revenue estimate; the ERP remains the canonical owner of the confirmed balance. Discrepancies between the two are a standard reconciliation pattern, not an error.
Revenue Recognition Relationships
The ASC 606 / IFRS 15 process of recognizing revenue over the service period.
Billing platforms with native revenue recognition (most notably Zuora) can contribute a Revenue Schedule; this never supersedes ERP-confirmed recognized revenue per the canonical ownership principle stated in Cross-Domain Relationships below.
Customer Payment History
The complete record of a customer's past payment behavior — on-time, late, failed, disputed.
A meaningful contributor to the composite canonical Health Score (Customer Success domain) and to Renewal Risk Analysis, but Billing is never the sole or authoritative source of Customer Health.
Cash Application
The process of matching a received payment to the specific invoice(s) it satisfies.
Relevant primarily for invoiced (non-card) payment methods; card payments are typically auto-applied. Cash Application accuracy affects AR Aging confidence.
Invoice Aging
The classification of unpaid invoices by how long they have been outstanding.
A standard AR Analytics concept; Billing-sourced aging should be cross-validated against ERP-sourced AR aging where both exist, since timing and write-off policies can diverge between the two systems.

The Governing Ownership Principle for Billing
Throughout this registry, the following ownership boundaries are maintained without exception:
Billing is not the accounting system. It does not determine recognized revenue. The ERP is the canonical owner of recognized revenue under ASC 606/IFRS 15, even when a Billing platform offers native revenue recognition scheduling.
Billing is not the CRM. It does not record sales intent, pipeline, or forecast. The CRM is the canonical owner of Opportunity and Pipeline Stage data, even when a Billing platform's CPQ-adjacent functionality (where present) touches similar concepts.
Billing does not own Customer Health. The Customer Success domain owns the composite Health Score. Billing contributes a partial signal — payment history, tenure, renewal proximity — never the full picture.
Billing validates recurring commercial execution. This is its canonical role: confirming that a subscription exists, is active, and is generating the ARR that CRM forecast and that ERP will eventually recognize as revenue.

Stripe Billing is the most widely deployed billing platform among SMPL's target customer base and offers the most consistent, best-documented API of any connector in this registry. It is frequently the first Billing connector implemented for a new SMPL customer.

Transformation Guidance
The Connector Engine treats Stripe as the authoritative source for Subscription state, ARR value, and Invoice/Payment data. It is never treated as the authoritative source for recognized revenue (ERP-owned) or confirmed bookings attribution (CRM-owned), per the Cross-Domain Relationships principles established for this registry. Stripe's service-period fields on every invoice make it one of the stronger Billing connectors for deferred revenue estimation, though the ERP remains canonically authoritative for the confirmed balance.

Bookings Validation

Stripe records what was actually billed, not what was sold — a Subscription confirms commercial execution, not sales attribution

CRM — the required pairing for this capability; Stripe alone can never independently confirm bookings

Executive Architecture Summary
Within the customer's broader system ecosystem, Stripe occupies the role of operational billing system of record — the most direct evidence of what customers are actually paying for, distinct from what was sold (CRM) and what has been recognized as revenue (ERP). Its contribution to Financial Intelligence is foundational: Stripe-derived ARR Movement data is the primary input to SMPL's ARR Waterfall and retention analytics across the majority of SMPL's customer base. Within the SCBM, Stripe populates the Subscription, Invoice, Payment, and ARR Movement canonical objects, and contributes a partial signal to the canonical Health Score.
Immediately after connection, Stripe delivers board-relevant ARR Reporting, MRR Reporting, Subscription Waterfalls, and Retention Analytics — sufficient on its own to give executive leadership SMPL's signature SaaS metrics with high confidence. The additional value unlocked when Stripe is combined with complementary systems is substantial: paired with ERP, Stripe-derived ARR becomes cross-validated against recognized revenue; paired with CRM, sales-recorded bookings become confirmed billing reality; paired with Banking, payment success becomes confirmed cash settlement. Stripe is frequently the foundation upon which a customer's full SMPL implementation is built, with every subsequent connector strengthening the picture Stripe alone has already begun to establish.

Canonical Mapping
ARR is derived by summing all active Rate Plan Charges across a Subscription's Rate Plans, annualized per billing frequency. Zuora's Amendment object provides an unusually well-structured source for ARR Movement classification — Amendment.type values (NewProduct, RemoveProduct, UpdateProduct, Renewal, Cancellation) map with high confidence to the canonical movement_category and movement_reason taxonomy established in the SCBM, more directly than the inferred-from-state-change patterns required by most other Billing connectors.
Transformation Guidance
The Connector Engine treats Zuora as authoritative for Subscription state and ARR value, and as the highest-confidence non-ERP contributor to Revenue Schedule in this registry given RevPro's enterprise-grade ASC 606/IFRS 15 functionality. Recommended Standard-Level Changes
Per the build brief's instruction, only changes that improve every future Connector Knowledge Registry are recommended here — no Billing-specific enhancements are proposed.
Confidence Drivers should be formally adopted as a required section in the permanent 22-section CKR standard, positioned immediately following Capability Progression. Every registry built under the standard computes confidence estimates; every registry would benefit from explaining them with the same rigor this section now provides for Billing. This is the more architecturally significant of the two v0.2 additions — it directly serves the SKA's explainability and auditability principles, not merely Billing's specific needs.
Implementation Decision Flow should be formally adopted as a required section in the permanent CKR standard, positioned after Implementation Risks and before Standalone Capability Summary. The business-oriented decision tree format is category-agnostic by construction — an ERP connector's flow would resolve Chart of Accounts and multi-entity questions, a CRM connector's flow would resolve stage-taxonomy and pipeline questions, in the same structural pattern demonstrated here for Billing's usage-policy and Rate Plan questions.
Both recommendations carry forward a pattern already established once before: the CRM Registry's v0.4 review recommended that Cross-Domain Relationships (introduced in this Billing Registry's v0.1) be retrofitted into the standard. That recommendation, combined with these two, means three sections beyond the original 22 are now candidates for permanent standard adoption: Cross-Domain Relationships, Confidence Drivers, and Implementation Decision Flow. A single consolidated standard-update pass — rather than three separate retrofits — is the efficient way to action all three before the next registry begins.
Items Explicitly Not Proposed as Standard Changes
The specific number of capabilities detailed in each connector's Confidence Drivers section (four to five representative capabilities per connector in this registry, rather than all twenty from the Capability Progression table) is a content-depth choice, not a structural requirement — future registries should select representative capabilities spanning their own category's confidence range, not mechanically replicate Billing's specific count.
Zuora's longer, more granular Implementation Decision Flow relative to Stripe's is a Zuora-specific characteristic correctly reflecting genuine complexity differences — not a standard-level requirement that every connector's flow reach a minimum length.
Final Determination
No further architectural improvements are identified. The Billing Connector Knowledge Registry is ready to be frozen as Version 1.0.
This is the third registry to reach freeze-ready status under the CKR Standard, following the ERP Registry (v1.0) and the CRM Registry (v1.0). Its v0.2 refinement followed the same pattern as the CRM Registry's own final iteration: targeted, well-scoped additions that strengthen explainability and implementation execution without touching any frozen architectural element, followed by a review that recommends standard-level adoption rather than treating the additions as Billing-specific improvements.
Confidence Drivers and Implementation Decision Flow, together with Cross-Domain Relationships (carried forward from this registry's v0.1 and already recommended for standard adoption by the CRM Registry's v0.4 review), now represent three consistent, well-tested candidates for permanent standard expansion. The recommended next step is a single consolidated CKR Standard update — formally elevating these three sections to the permanent 22-section (becoming 25-section) standard — performed once, before the next connector category registry begins, rather than as a repeated per-registry retrofit pattern.
The Billing Connector Knowledge Registry, as it stands at the conclusion of this review, is frozen as Version 1.0.
