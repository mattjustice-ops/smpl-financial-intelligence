What Defines an HRIS Platform
A Human Resources Information System is the operational system of record for how an organization's people are structured, employed, compensated, and managed. Where ERP explains financial truth, CRM explains commercial intent, and Billing explains commercial execution, HRIS explains workforce execution: who works at the company, in what role, reporting to whom, at what compensation, and with what employment status.
Every HRIS platform in this registry maintains, at minimum, an Employee object, a Department or organizational unit structure, a Position or Job object defining roles, and a reporting relationship hierarchy. Beyond this core, HRIS platforms extend into compensation management, position and requisition planning, leave and absence tracking, and — in the most comprehensive platforms — workforce planning and organizational design functionality that overlaps meaningfully with FP&A.
For SMPL, HRIS is the primary connector category for Workforce Planning, Headcount Reporting, and Department-level expense forecasting. No ERP can produce trustworthy headcount or organizational structure data — ERPs record GL-level payroll postings, not individual employee records or reporting relationships. No Payroll system, while authoritative for compensation amounts actually paid, typically owns the organizational hierarchy, job architecture, or position planning data that HRIS does. HRIS is the connector category that answers, with operational authority: who works here, in what structure, and what is the organization planning to look like going forward?
HRIS vs. Payroll — Where the Boundary Sits
This is the single most consequential boundary in this registry, and it is frequently a source of customer confusion since many vendors (Rippling, ADP Workforce Now, UKG Pro) offer combined HRIS-and-Payroll product suites. HRIS owns the organizational record: who is employed, in what role, in what department, reporting to whom, and what their target compensation is. Payroll owns the execution record: what was actually paid, when, with what tax withholding and deductions, in a specific pay run.
The distinction matters architecturally because the two domains have different canonical objects and different confidence profiles. An Employee's base_salary field in HRIS represents the compensation plan; a Payroll Line's gross_pay field represents what was actually disbursed in a specific period. These are related but distinct figures — a mid-cycle raise, a one-time bonus, or a payroll error can cause them to diverge temporarily, and SMPL must never conflate the two. Per the Cross-Domain Relationships section below, HRIS is the canonical owner of the organizational and compensation-plan record; Payroll (where connected as a distinct system, or as a module within a combined HRIS+Payroll platform) is the canonical owner of actual disbursement.
HRIS vs. ERP — Where the Boundary Sits
HRIS owns headcount and organizational structure; ERP owns the financial consequence of that structure once it is posted to the General Ledger. An employee's existence, department assignment, and compensation plan are HRIS facts. The GL journal entries generated by payroll runs, and the resulting Department-level P&L expense, are ERP facts. HRIS-derived headcount and compensation data is the leading indicator; ERP-confirmed payroll expense is the lagging, recognized confirmation — both are necessary for trusted Workforce Planning and Labor Cost Forecasting, neither substitutes for the other.
HRIS vs. Recruiting — Where the Boundary Sits
HRIS owns the employed workforce; dedicated Recruiting or Applicant Tracking Systems (Greenhouse, Lever, and similar — out of scope for this registry but referenced in Future Registry Expansion) own the candidate pipeline before hire. Most HRIS platforms in this registry expose Open Requisition and Scheduled Hire data representing planned future headcount, but this is materially lower-confidence than confirmed Employee records, and is never treated as equivalent to it.
Workforce Lifecycle
Every HRIS platform models an Employee moving through a defined lifecycle: Requisition (planned, not yet filled) → Hire → Active (with possible Promotions, Transfers, and Leaves of Absence along the way) → Termination. The specific state names and workflow steps vary by vendor, but this general lifecycle shape is universal across every platform in this registry.
Hiring lifecycle: an Open Requisition is approved, a Scheduled Hire is recorded with an expected start date, and a Hire event creates the Employee record on or near the actual start date.
Promotions and transfers: a change in an Employee's Job, Position, compensation band, or Department/reporting line without an intervening termination — the HRIS-side trigger for internal mobility analysis.
Leaves of absence: a temporary, defined-duration suspension of active work status (parental leave, medical leave, sabbatical) without termination — relevant for accurate headcount-vs-FTE distinction during the leave period.
Terminations: the end of an employment relationship, typically classified as voluntary or involuntary — the HRIS-side trigger for Attrition Analysis.
Contractor management: many HRIS platforms track contractors and contingent workers alongside full-time employees, typically with a distinct Employment Type classification that affects FTE calculation and headcount reporting.
Organizational Hierarchy and Manager Hierarchy
Department, Cost Center, Business Unit, and Division represent the organizational classification dimensions used for headcount and expense reporting — conceptually similar to the Department dimension already established in the ERP Registry, but HRIS is typically the canonical source for the org structure itself, with ERP's cost center mapping derived from it. Manager Hierarchy — the reporting-relationship chain from any Employee up to the CEO — is a distinct dimension from Department, used for span-of-control analysis and approval routing, and is documented per connector since manager-hierarchy data completeness varies meaningfully across the five platforms in this registry.
Job Architecture
Job, Job Family, and Position represent the role-classification layer: Job Family groups related roles (e.g., "Engineering"), Job represents a specific role definition within that family (e.g., "Senior Software Engineer"), and Position represents a specific instance of that Job that can be filled, vacant, or planned within the org structure. Job architecture sophistication varies significantly across the five platforms in this registry, from Workday's formal, configurable job architecture to simpler title-based models in platforms positioned for smaller organizations.
Compensation
Base Salary, Variable Compensation, Bonus Plans, and Equity Grants (referenced, not canonically owned by HRIS — equity administration platforms like Carta are the canonical source) together represent an Employee's total compensation plan. HRIS-recorded compensation is the planned/target figure; actual disbursed compensation is a Payroll fact, per the HRIS-vs-Payroll boundary established above.
Workforce Planning and Position Management
The most financially consequential HRIS functionality for SMPL: Budgeted Positions (planned headcount, whether filled or not), Filled Positions (current headcount), and Open Positions (approved but unfilled) together support Vacancy Analysis and Hiring Forecast. Workforce Plan objects, where present, represent forward-looking headcount and compensation planning that — like Budget Line in the ERP Registry — requires Scenario-level versioning (Actual vs. Plan) to be used correctly in canonical reasoning.

The following business concepts are common across every HRIS platform in this registry but require explicit disambiguation, consistent with the Business Concept Registry treatment established in the SKA and applied in the prior three registries.

Workforce Planning
The forward-looking process of planning headcount and compensation by department and role.
Distinct from payroll processing or accounting — Workforce Planning is a planning activity, with actuals reconciled against it after the fact, structurally analogous to Budget vs. Actual in the ERP Registry.
Headcount
A point-in-time count of active employees.
Whether contractors are included, and whether the count is as-of a specific date or averaged over a period, are both CEP-configurable policies that materially affect the reported figure.
FTE
Full-Time-Equivalent — a normalized headcount figure accounting for part-time work.
The conversion methodology (e.g., is a 0.5 FTE part-time employee counted as 0.5, or does the platform use a different fractional convention?) varies by vendor and customer policy — never assumed, always CEP-confirmed.
Compensation Planning
The process of setting and adjusting planned compensation, typically through Merit Cycles.
Planned compensation (HRIS) vs. actually disbursed compensation (Payroll) are different figures that should agree over time but can diverge temporarily — never conflated.
Hiring Plan
A forward-looking plan for headcount growth by role and department.
Distinguished from current Open Requisitions — a Hiring Plan may include positions not yet formally requisitioned, representing earlier-stage planning intent.
Attrition
The rate at which employees leave the organization, typically expressed as an annualized percentage.
Voluntary vs. involuntary attrition should be distinguished where source data supports it — these represent different business problems requiring different interventions, structurally analogous to the voluntary/involuntary churn distinction in the Billing Registry.
Internal Mobility
The rate and pattern of employees moving between roles or departments without leaving the organization.
Distinguished from both Hiring (external) and Attrition (departure) — internal mobility is a retention-positive signal that some platforms track explicitly and others require derivation from Transfer/Promotion event history.
Organizational Design
The structure of departments, reporting lines, and span of control across the organization.
A point-in-time snapshot concept — organizational design changes over time, and historical org structure queries require the same point-in-time reasoning capability the SKA establishes for all canonical objects.
Span of Control
The number of direct reports a manager has.
A derived metric from the reporting relationship chain, not independently stored — computed from Employee.reports_to_employee_id, consistent with the principle that metrics are computed, not sourced pre-computed from any connector.
Cost Center Allocation
The mapping of employee compensation cost to financial reporting dimensions.
Cost Center is sometimes, but not always, identical to Department — the mapping between the two (1:1, many:1, or independent dimension) is a CEP-configurable decision specific to each customer's chart of accounts design.
Department Planning
Forward-looking headcount and budget planning at the department level.
The HRIS-side planning figure (Workforce Plan) and the ERP-side Budget Line for department payroll expense should reconcile but are recorded in different systems and may use different period or currency conventions.
Salary Bands
A defined compensation range associated with a Job or Job Level.
Band structure sophistication varies enormously across the five platforms in this registry, from Workday's formal, multi-dimensional band architecture to simpler or absent band structures in platforms positioned for smaller organizations.
Merit Cycles
A periodic, organization-wide compensation review and adjustment process.
Merit cycle timing and structure is entirely customer-configured — never assumed to follow a calendar-year or any other default cadence.
Workforce Forecasting
Projecting future headcount and labor cost based on current trends and planned hiring.
Requires both HRIS (current state and plan) and ERP (realized labor cost) for full confidence — an HRIS-only forecast can project headcount but not fully validate the resulting financial cost.
Budgeted vs. Filled vs. Open Positions
The three-way state of a Position: planned but not yet active, currently filled, or approved-but-vacant.
This three-way distinction is the foundation of Vacancy Analysis and is more reliably available in platforms with formal Position Management functionality (Workday) than in simpler platforms that model headcount primarily through filled Employee records.
Vacancy Analysis
The identification and aging of open, unfilled positions.
Requires Position Management functionality specifically — platforms without a formal Position object can only approximate this from Open Requisition data, which is a less structured proxy.
Organizational Changes
Department restructuring, reporting line changes, or business unit reorganization.
Historical organizational change tracking varies significantly by platform — some maintain a full audit history of org changes, others reflect only the current state.
Labor Cost Planning
Forward-looking financial planning specifically for compensation and headcount-driven expense.
The most consequential cross-domain workforce concept in this registry — requires HRIS for headcount and compensation plan, Payroll for actual disbursement patterns (including employer taxes and benefits load), and ERP for GL-level expense recognition.

The Governing Ownership Principle for HRIS
Throughout this registry, the following ownership boundaries are maintained without exception:
HRIS is not Payroll. It does not own actual disbursed compensation, tax withholding, or benefits administration. Payroll (whether a logically distinct system or a module within a combined platform) is the canonical owner of execution-level compensation facts.
HRIS is not ERP. It does not own recognized payroll expense or the Chart of Accounts. The ERP is the canonical owner of GL-level financial reporting, even when HRIS provides the organizational structure that GL expense allocation depends on.
HRIS is not Recruiting. It does not own candidate pipeline data prior to hire. A dedicated Applicant Tracking System, where connected, is the canonical owner of pre-hire candidate data — HRIS's Requisition and Scheduled Hire objects represent planned headcount, not candidate-stage detail.
HRIS contributes workforce intelligence. This is its canonical role: confirming who is employed, in what structure, at what planned compensation, supporting Workforce Planning, Headcount Reporting, and Labor Cost Forecasting in combination with Payroll and ERP confirmation.

Transformation Guidance
The Connector Engine treats Workday as authoritative for organizational structure, Position status, and planned compensation. It is never treated as the authoritative source for actual disbursed compensation (Payroll-owned, even when Workday Payroll is the connected module) or recognized payroll expense (ERP-owned), per the Cross-Domain Relationships principles established for this registry.

Executive Architecture Summary
Within the customer's broader system ecosystem, ADP Workforce Now occupies the operational workforce system-of-record role with a distinctive strength in payroll-tax and compliance depth inherited from ADP's payroll-processing origins. Its contribution to Financial Intelligence is strongest specifically in Compensation Planning and Fully-Loaded Cost per Employee — the most complete payroll-tax-inclusive cost data of any connector in this registry. Within the SCBM, it populates Employee, a more customer-variable Department structure, and unusually deep Payroll Line data.
Immediately after connection, ADP delivers reliable Workforce Planning, Headcount Reporting, Compensation Planning, and Fully-Loaded Cost per Employee. The additional value unlocked through ERP pairing is particularly significant for Department Reporting specifically, given ADP's more variable native organizational structure relative to purpose-built modern HRIS platforms.

Standard-Level Finding: Implementation Decision Flow Should Now Be Formally Confirmed
This build brief's Connector Specifications list explicitly requested Confidence Drivers — confirming its adoption as permanent standard content, as recommended by the Billing Registry v0.2 review. It did not explicitly request Implementation Decision Flow. This registry included it anyway, on the basis stated in the Scope Note at the start of this document, and that inclusion is revisited here rather than left as a silent assumption.
Having now built Implementation Decision Flow for a second registry — HRIS, with a meaningfully different decision structure than Billing's usage-policy and Rate Plan questions (HRIS's flow centers on organizational-system-of-record confirmation, Payroll module activation, and Position Management configuration) — the section has demonstrated category-agnostic value twice, not once. This strengthens, rather than weakens, the case for its formal adoption.
Recommendation: Implementation Decision Flow should be formally and permanently added to the CKR standard's required section list, alongside Cross-Domain Relationships and Confidence Drivers. This closes the loop opened by the Billing Registry v0.2 review, which recommended adoption but left the decision pending a second registry's validation. That validation is now complete.
No Other Architectural Gaps Identified
Per the build brief's instruction to identify only improvements that strengthen the permanent CKR standard, no other gaps are identified. The HRIS-specific characteristics observed in this registry — Workday's formal Position Management, ADP's payroll-tax depth, BambooHR's flat organizational model, the three-way HRIS/Payroll/ERP compensation distinction — are all HRIS-specific content correctly captured within the existing standard structure, not gaps in the structure itself.
Final Determination
If a prospective customer told SMPL they use one of these five HRIS platforms, does this registry provide sufficient implementation knowledge for AI implementation agents and human implementation teams to consistently onboard the platform, accurately populate the SCBM, support the Capability Assessment Layer, and deliver trusted Financial Intelligence?
Yes — for all five connectors, with maturity- and platform-design-appropriate confidence framing that differs meaningfully and honestly by connector, consistent with the standard now validated across four registries.
For consistency of onboarding: every connector follows the identical structure, validated now across ERP, CRM, Billing, and HRIS — four fundamentally different underlying data models (GL-centric, pipeline-centric, subscription-lifecycle-centric, and now organizational-hierarchy-centric) all accommodated by the same standard without modification. This is the strongest cumulative evidence yet that the standard is durable.
For accurate Capability Assessment: Capability Progression and Confidence Drivers are populated for all five connectors, with differentiation that reflects genuine, explainable differences — Workday's Position Management strength, ADP's payroll-tax strength, BambooHR's structural simplicity, each correctly and distinctly characterized rather than flattened into a uniform HRIS confidence profile.
For trusted Financial Intelligence specifically: the structural caveat established across every prior registry holds here with a notable addition — HRIS introduces a three-way distinction (planned compensation in HRIS, actual disbursement in Payroll, recognized expense in ERP) that is more complex than the two-way CRM-vs-ERP or Billing-vs-ERP distinctions in prior registries. No HRIS connector in this registry, regardless of maturity or native Payroll integration sophistication, fully substitutes for ERP-confirmed recognized payroll expense — even Rippling and UKG Pro with native Payroll modules active remain explicitly subordinate to ERP for GL-level confirmation. Trusted Workforce Financial Intelligence requires the three-way combination this registry consistently recommends: HRIS for organizational and planned-compensation truth, Payroll for disbursement confirmation, ERP for recognized-expense confirmation.
What remains before declaring full production validation, not architectural completion: the same gap named consistently across all four registries — confidence figures throughout this registry are SMPL's best architecturally-derived planning estimates, not yet confirmed against production Capability Assessment Layer output at scale, particularly for the three Validated-maturity connectors (Workday, UKG Pro, ADP). This is stated explicitly throughout via the standardized Planning Guidance section.
The HRIS Connector Knowledge Registry, as it stands at the conclusion of this review, is ready to be frozen as Version 1.0 — the fourth registry to reach this status under the CKR Standard, and the registry that completes the validation case for Implementation Decision Flow's formal standard adoption.
